The Culture

More than cars. A way of life shaped by touge runs, car meets, and decades of motorsport heritage.

Origins

What is JDM?

The term "JDM" (Japanese Domestic Market) originally referred to vehicles produced and sold exclusively within Japan. These cars were often tuned to stricter emissions laws, voluntary power agreements, and the unique demands of Japanese roads.

Over time, the term evolved into something bigger — a cultural movement embracing the aesthetics, performance, and spirit of Japanese car culture, from wide-body Silvias to stock R34 GT-Rs. Now it is a global identity.

Touge

Mountain pass racing culture — the spiritual home of JDM driving. Winding roads, inches from the guardrail, mechanical skill over horsepower.

Wangan

The expressway legends. Straight-line midnight runs on Tokyo's waterfront highways where top speed is everything.

D1 Grand Prix

Japan's elite drifting championship. Loud, sideways, smoky — the most theatrical motorsport series on the planet.
